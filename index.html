THE HELION COMPACT

Hard-political science fiction. Ice, intent, and the crime already under the floor.

A Core technician who can hear a dying plant — and the Compact that taught him not to hear people.

On Hearth Station the meters stay green while the water dies. Cade Voss logs the truth, then edits the official language, because that is what useful men do. When a bypass he failed to name kills someone in the next compartment, exile arrives dressed as honor.

LOW WATER opens a seven-book cycle about discipline, scarce heat, and the crime that was taught as virtue.

Who this is for

LOW WATER is for readers who want a hard-political thriller that earns its heat. Scarce heat and the usefulness trap: what you can still do for other people is the last currency that matters.

If you prefer exile-as-honor over chosen-one swagger, delayed-burn tension over spectacle, and a protagonist who must stay useful without becoming owned — start here.
